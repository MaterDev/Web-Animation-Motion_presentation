layouts

Every slide is content on a pixelated LED panel, composed on a modular grid quantised to that panel's own matrix. Two ideas that only work together: Swedish modernist layout discipline gives the compositions their structure, and the LED matrix gives that structure a reason to land on exactly these numbers rather than arbitrary ones.

The grid — 12 × 6 square modules on a 160 × 90 matrix

The slide is a 960 × 540 panel of 6px LED cells — a 160 × 90 matrix. Margins take 8 cells at the sides and 9 top and bottom, leaving a field of 144 × 72 cells that divides exactly into 12 columns × 6 rows of 12 × 12-cell squares. Every module is a true square, every edge lands on a cell boundary, and nothing in a composition can fall between pixels. Toggle the guides to see the field against a real slide.

The margin is composition, not leftover. 8 and 9 cells is a lot of empty edge for a 16:9 frame, and that's the Swedish part: the void is an active element that gives the type somewhere to sit rather than space nobody got round to using. Only a deliberate full-bleed layer is allowed to cross it.
Why no gutter

Columns butt directly against each other and spacing is made by leaving a module empty, not by a gutter value. It means every gap in every composition is exactly one module wide — the same distance everywhere, guaranteed by the structure rather than by remembering to type the same number. It also keeps the arithmetic whole: a gutter would put fractional cells between columns and break the one rule the whole system rests on.

The type scale — five sizes, all whole cells

Sizes are 12, 8, 4, 3 and 2 cells, with line-heights in whole cells too, so successive baselines stay on matrix rows. Display and head sizes are set in Doto, the dot-matrix face — at those sizes the type is literally built from the same pixels as the panel it sits on. Below that a dot face stops being legible at projection distance, so it hands off to mono and sans. Five sizes is the entire scale; the restriction is the point.
