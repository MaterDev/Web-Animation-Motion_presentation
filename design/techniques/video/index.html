SL-04

video

The baseline, and the cheapest way to put motion on a screen. Pre-baked pixels with a real element around them — and the only pre-baked format that can be told to stop.

MP4

the broadcast

Video is the format built for continuous tone. A real codec spends its bits on change between frames rather than re-describing every pixel, so gradients, soft shadow falloff and specular rolloff cost almost nothing to carry — and the longer a shot holds still, the cheaper it gets. It also arrives with a real element: <video> can be paused, scrubbed, muted, rate-changed, decoded into a canvas, and — the one that matters most for slide 15 — told to stop for prefers-reduced-motion from actual JS.

The subject is chosen to make that case rather than assert it: the NOVA-7, a fictional handheld console, shot on a studio backdrop under one key and one fill. The loop is a reveal with beats rather than a continuous wobble — hold on the assembled unit, separate into layers, hold open long enough to read the internals, reassemble, settle. Moulded ABS, rubber d-pad, glossy button caps, brushed aluminium shielding, matte battery wrap and solder-mask green are exactly the kind of continuous gradient a codec carries well. Baked from gen/bake-video.mjs — a software rasteriser with a depth buffer, per-pixel Fresnel, a procedural studio environment sampled along the reflection vector, and 3× supersampling. The loop is exact by construction: both holds rest at a stationary value, so the wrap from last frame to first is not merely continuous but motionless.

TD-VIDEO

the decoded frame is a buffer

A baked file is immutable. The buffer it decodes into is not — and that is the whole of this section. Every effect in the rack reads the frame the decoder just produced, rearranges it, and writes it back out: bands sampled from different stored frames, blocks held over from earlier ones, pixels flattened into mosaic patches, the canvas fed its own last output. None of it touches the .mp4. Alongside them sit the declarative filters, which hand a graph to the compositor instead — the same picture arrived at from the other end, and worth being able to tell apart. Flip camera and the identical rack runs on a live stream, because the effects operate on the decoded frame and the frame does not care where it came from.

The surface it runs on is the vertical cut — generated at 720×1280, not cropped from the landscape master, so the camera reframes rather than the edges being thrown away, which is the honest version of slide 13's "same tech, different surfaces" claim. Its controls are hand-built for the vertical idiom: tap anywhere, thin progress, mute — every one of them scripting the same element the player above exposes natively.

CHROMA KEY

an alpha channel the file never had

.mp4 in a browser has no alpha — H.264 in the profiles anything ships carries three channels and no fourth. So a subject in a video is stuck inside its rectangle, and every "video floating on the page" you have seen either paid for HEVC-with-alpha and shipped a WebM fallback, or did this: shot the subject on a flat colour and computed the missing channel per frame. The decode goes to a canvas, every pixel's chroma is measured against the key, the distance becomes alpha, and green bleeding onto the edges is pulled back out. What comes out the other side is an element with real transparency that page furniture can sit in front of and behind. Pick a scene — the same keyed frames, composited into different web graphics.
