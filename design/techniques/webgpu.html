webgpu

The GPU stops being a place you send pictures to and becomes a place things happen. Compute passes read and write storage buffers that never come back; a hundred thousand grains can be an interface, a flock can be a game, a world can be grown and flown over, a product shot can be lit without a product — and the app around each of those can be the GPU's work too. One device, eight apps, one canvas, each a fragment of a product that could ship — because a technique on a coloured square teaches nothing about where you would reach for it.

Every number on this sheet is measured on this machine, now. The GPU writes its own clock at the start and end of each pass (timestamp-query), so cards print passes, not frames. A counter on the device queue records every byte the CPU sends per frame. Adapter … · timestamp-query … · state uploaded this frame … · uniforms … · backing store …

Pipeline

what a frame costs on the GPU

A frame here is a list of passes. Each compute pass runs one program across a grid of workgroups; the render pass draws. Validation happens once, when the pipeline is created, so a pass costs what its work costs and nothing else — and because the GPU timestamps the start and end of every pass, the cost is a measurement rather than a guess. Switch passes off and watch the bars: the scene loses the pass's effect and the frame loses exactly its time.

One device · eight apps · one canvas

WebGPU treated as an app, not a demo

The oldest WebGPU demo is a particle system, and it is usually a screensaver. The argument here is that the GPU can be the whole app: below is one iPhone Duo, and everything inside its display — the panes, the type, the glass, the buttons, the cards, the sand, the honey, the flock, the world, the shoe, the storm and the phone's own portrait — is drawn by WebGPU from one canvas. The phone around it is CSS, there only to say "this form factor". The Dock opens one app at a time; the last one is torn down before the next compiles, so a single set of buffers is ever alive. Text is glyphs in an atlas, panels are signed distances, and frosted glass samples a blurred copy of whatever the app drew behind it.

What this sheet is holding itself to

  1. Name the pass, not the frame. Every GPU time printed here is one named pass between two timestamps.
  2. The zero is queried. Bytes per frame come from a counter on the device queue; the pointer and the uniforms are what crosses, and they are printed rather than rounded away.
  3. A readback is a stall with a number. Where a card asks the GPU a question — a fragment count, five heights — it prints what the asking cost.
  4. Raw WGSL, no library. The storage buffers, the workgroups, the atomics and the sort are the argument — and so is the interface: the text atlas, the signed-distance panels and the frosted glass in every app are a hundred lines of WGSL, not a framework.
  5. Every product is invented. Atlas, Nectar, the eight issuers in the wallet and their scheme, Roost, Tessera, Halcyon and Supercell do not exist. The iPhone Duo is Apple's; the model's dimensions are labelled verified, derived or constructed on the card. The physics, the rule after Ballerini et al. 2008 and every measurement are real.
