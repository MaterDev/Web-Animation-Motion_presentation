the idea

00

Three consequences of a mid-grey ground and a locked two-accent palette.

1 · Mid grey gives you both directions. This is the structural advantage over T-01 and T-02. From a light base, surfaces can only rise; from a dark base, only fall. From the middle, panels rise and display wells sink — so the chassis reads as a physical object with things mounted in it, not as a backdrop.
2 · The section ramp becomes temperature, not rainbow. With blue and amber locked as the accent pair, seven arbitrary hues would fight them. The spectrum runs cold → hot instead, so the escalation still reads and it matches the way real instruments signal load. WebGPU is where the meter goes amber.
3 · Silkscreen becomes available. On a mid-grey shell, light label ink is physically right — it's how every device in the reference set is actually marked. It only works at label scale, which is a useful constraint rather than a limit.

Four things make it read as a real display, and all four are cheap: a pixel cell grid over the field, a diagonal polariser sheen, backlight bleed at the top edge, and the dead segments ghosted behind the live figures. Remove any one and it degrades into a coloured box with mono text in it.
Real LCDs are low-contrast — that's the trap. The aesthetic is authentic precisely because segment ink barely separates from the field, which is exactly what fails from the back of a room. Use displays for chrome — timers, codes, status — and never for anything the audience has to read. Presenter-facing is fine; the presenter is 400mm from the screen.

The honest cost of a mid-grey ground. You get two text directions but neither has full range — dark text tops out lower than it would on white, light text tops out lower than on black. Dark text from --hz-500 down is safe; light text is label scale only. Do not set body copy in silkscreen.

Accent pair

--blue · oklch(0.720 0.125 232)
Active, current, powered. The default state colour — backlit displays, the progress fill, primary controls.
--amber · oklch(0.800 0.155 85)
Load, caution, peak. Over-time pacing, the WebGPU end of the ramp, anything running hot.
The pair is a scale, not two colours. Blue means nominal, amber means loaded — so a single element can travel between them to express intensity. The progress rail and the section ramp both do exactly this.

Section ramp — cold to hot · click to reskin

The ramp's weakness is its middle. Cold and hot ends are unmistakable, but SVG-to-Canvas is a small hue step and won't be read as "one rung up" on its own VERIFY. Section names carry the identification; the ramp only carries the direction of travel. Test it by clicking through in order.

Signal

--signal · oklch(0.505 0.215 27)

Now competing with amber, so it must stay genuinely rare. Amber is "running hot", red is "something is wrong" — if amber is doing its job, red should almost never fire.

One motion idea this treatment unlocks. LCD segments don't fade — they switch. Any value inside a display should step discretely rather than tween, using --ease-mechanical or no transition at all. The contrast between smoothly animating chrome and hard-switching readouts is what sells both.
