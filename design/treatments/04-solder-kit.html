solder kit

T-03 with the saturation taken out and the workbench put in. Displays are unlit and reflective rather than backlit; the shell has aged; tone comes from ordered dither instead of gradients; and the whole thing sits on perfboard.

ReferenceGBA (unlit) · Heathkit · perfboard · through-hole

the idea

00
1 · Saturation is what reads as "new". Chroma is roughly halved from T-03. Vintage pigment fades, plastic yellows under UV, and phosphor dims — so the shell is warm-shifted rather than neutral grey, because a perfectly neutral grey reads as modern injection moulding.
2 · The screen is reflective, not backlit. This is the actual difference between a GBA and a GBA SP, and it's the single biggest change from T-03. Light arrives from the front, so there's no glow at the top edge — just a broad diagonal reflection off the glass, and genuinely poor contrast underneath it.
3 · Dither replaces gradients. A limited palette can't ramp smoothly, so tone comes from ordered dithering. This is the strongest idea in the treatment, because it's honest about the constraint in exactly the way exposed electronics are — and dithering is itself a rendering technique, which puts it on-thesis for the talk.
4 · Corners go square. Radii drop to 2px or zero and shadows become hard offsets rather than soft blurs. Pixel art has no antialiasing; soft radii next to a dither pattern read as a mistake.

Dither as a tone ramp

One hue, five tones, no gradient — this is how shading works in this treatment.

Why this is the good idea. Dither makes the constraint visible in the surface itself — the same move as a transparent case showing you the board. It's also, at 1-2px, effectively free, and it survives projection better than a subtle gradient does, since projectors band smooth ramps badly.
One real cost. Fine dither can shimmer or moiré when scaled or moved, and it will alias against a projector's native resolution VERIFY. Keep dithered areas static, or scale them in whole pixels. Never dither something that animates continuously.

Accent pair — muted

--blue · oklch(0.585 0.058 232)
Slate, not sky. Chroma 0.058 against T-03's 0.125 — the difference between painted metal and a lit LED.
--amber · oklch(0.688 0.079 78)
Ochre, not yellow. Reads as aged plastic and old warning labels rather than as a highlighter.

Section ramp — colour bands

Presented as a resistor, because a resistor is the thing in every kit that encodes a value as bands of colour. Click a band to reskin.

Muting costs separation. At this chroma the seven steps are much closer together than in T-03, and the middle of the ramp is now very weak VERIFY. If the section colour has to be identifiable on its own rather than just directional, this treatment needs either fewer sections or a second cue — the dither density, for instance, could step with the ramp.

Signal

--signal · oklch(0.470 0.140 30)

Oxidised red — closer to a warning label that's been on a machine for thirty years than to an alert state. Still the most saturated thing in the system, which is what keeps it working.

Stepped motion is this treatment's signature. Low-framerate handheld hardware moved sprites in whole pixels at a fixed tick, and steps() reproduces that exactly. Use it for readouts, meters and counters — never for slide transitions, where it just reads as dropped frames.
