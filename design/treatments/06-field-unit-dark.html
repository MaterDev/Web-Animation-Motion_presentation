materials

Eleven surfaces, nine of them distinct manufacturing processes. Hover any swatch — the specular sweep is how a real surface announces it's tilted into the light.

"Aluminium" is not one finish — it's a tool direction. Anodised brush runs linear, turned runs concentric, milled leaves parallel channels. Same metal, same colour, and they read as completely different parts. Anodised dye is the one that earns its place functionally: the dye sinks into the oxide layer so the grain still shows through, which is exactly what separates anodising from paint — and it means accent parts can carry --tint while still reading as metal.
The chamfer is the highest-value detail here. A bright hairline around a machined edge is the single most recognisable Teenage Engineering signature, and it costs one inset ring plus a directional highlight. Applied to the hero panel above.
Material must follow function or it's costume. Anodised for the chassis, blasted for secondary panels, knurled only where a hand would grip, rubber only where the unit touches a surface. Applied decoratively, six materials look worse than one.
01

glass

Fig. 2

Earlier sheets rejected "glassmorphism" and that rejection stands — for the flat version. Real glass does four things the cliché doesn't: it refracts what's behind it, lenses hardest at the rim, throws caustics along the edge, and disperses colour. All four are below. Hover the fourth.

01 · Flat blur
The cliché. One blur, one even border.
02 · Optical
Rim lensing, caustics, dispersion. CSS only.
03 · Refractive
Backdrop actually displaced. Grid bends.
04 · Liquid
Hover — radius morphs, body swells.
The grid behind the panels is the test. Straight rules are the only honest way to show refraction: under 01 they stay straight and merely blur, under 03 they bend. If a glass treatment can't visibly distort a straight line, it isn't glass — it's a translucent rectangle.
Glass needs a subject, and that's the real cost. Every panel here sits on a deliberately busy bed. Over flat grey it collapses back into the cliché no matter how good the optics are. For this deck that's actually fine — the demos are behind the chrome — but it means glass can only be used where something is genuinely running underneath, never as a default panel style.
Cost check. backdrop-filter forces the compositor to re-sample everything beneath, and the SVG displacement doubles that. Three of these on screen is fine; one over a live WebGPU particle sim is not. Rule for the build: glass never overlaps a running demo — it goes on chrome that sits beside one.
02

liquid glass motion

Fig. 3 · Apple, WWDC 2025

Not water physics — Apple's actual system, shipped as "Liquid Glass" across iOS/macOS/iPadOS 26. Sources: Apple Newsroom, MacStories. It has almost nothing to do with the blob/ripple/tank build that was here before this pass — that was a literal reading of "liquid" that doesn't match what Apple built. Four things define Apple's own system, and only the last one overlaps with what was here already. A fifth, below, is this system's own extension of it — the same curve, looped ambiently rather than fired once, because it turned out to be the only motion vocabulary the rest of this design system needed.

A · Morph
Trigger click · 420ms · liquid
The control becomes the panel — width, height and radius animate together, nothing is swapped or replaced. Apple: "the element expands organically rather than just moving."
B · Specular tilt — move your cursor over it
Trigger pointer move · continuous
Highlight tracks motion in real time — device accelerometer on hardware, cursor position standing in for it here. Not a fixed gradient; the light genuinely moves with you.
Keyboard: deliberately none. This is ambient material response layered on top of other content, not a control — nothing here is only reachable this way, so unlike the knob/toggle/morph it doesn't need a keyboard path; it already has a sensible static rest state.
C · Concentric radius
Formula r = outer − padding, always
Every nested radius is computed, never eyeballed — calc(var(--r) - var(--p)). A fixed inner radius next to a rounder outer one is the single most common tell of a UI that copied the look without the system.
D · Spring indicator
Trigger click · 420ms · liquid
Resists leaving, catches up, settles without oscillating. The one place the earlier build was already right — Apple's motion is spring-based throughout, and --ease-liquid already approximated that correctly.
E · Ambient idle
Trigger none · continuous · 2.2s · liquid
Same curve, looped instead of fired once — settles, slightly overshoots, never fully stops. This is the only ambient/decorative motion used anywhere in this design system: every idle loop on every page, whatever it's attached to, runs this exact curve via the shared .liquid-idle/.liquid-fill classes in components.css. The Graphic Language reference page is the clearest example — seven topics, one shared motion, differentiated entirely by shape, colour and pattern.
What survives from the first pass: exactly one curve. --ease-liquid and the spring/settle idea behind it were correct — Apple's system really is spring-driven. Everything built around that curve was wrong: metaball fusion, a contact ripple, and a liquid-filled tank are water-simulation ideas with no counterpart in Liquid Glass. They've been removed rather than kept as extras, so this section doesn't quietly teach the wrong thing.
Where morph does not belong. Slide advance, for the same reason as before — it happens 200+ times before delivery, and a shape-morph at that frequency is the exact failure mode SCOPE.md names. Morph is for moments of arrival: a control opening into its own detail, not routine navigation.
One curve, not a palette to pick from per context. It would be easy to read "differentiate each topic/section" as licence to give each one its own timing or easing — a slightly bouncier curve here, a snappier one there. That's exactly what this system rules out: motion stays identical everywhere it appears, and differentiation is carried entirely by graphic elements — colour, shape, pattern, type — never by bespoke motion per instance. A reader shouldn't be able to tell two contexts apart by how something moves, only by how it looks.

micro-animations

Fig. 4 · 12 entries

Every one is live. Triggers are marked — hover, click, or ambient. Durations and easing are stated so they can be implemented without guessing.

01 · Key press
Trigger :active · 90ms · standard
Key travels 3px AND its stand-off collapses. Two properties, one gesture — travel alone reads as a slide, not a press.

Slight overshoot then settle. The only place overshoot is honest in this system — a sprung detent genuinely does this.

05 · Response smear
Trigger value change · 180ms · mechanical
Real LCD segments drag as they switch. One frame of blur and 1px offset — the detail that makes a readout feel physical.
06 · LED power-on
Trigger boot · 520ms · decelerate
Staggered 90ms apart. Sequencing reads as a system coming up; simultaneous reads as a CSS class toggling.

Glow radius only, never opacity — a real indicator doesn't dim, its bloom changes. Stops under reduced motion.

Trigger error · 900ms · steps(1)
Hard on/off, ~1.1Hz. Well under the 3Hz seizure threshold in WCAG 2.3.1 — worth stating, since blink is the easiest way to breach it.

Trigger value · 900ms · ballistic
Damped arrival, no overshoot. A meter that bounces reads as a toy; a needle settles.

Ambient and triggered are different categories. Only two entries here loop (07, 11's idle state) and both are decorative. Everything else fires once in response to something the user did — which is why the whole catalogue can be switched off without losing information.

Three curves added
CurveValueUse
--ease-standard0.2,0,0,1Default. Slide advance, most state change.
--ease-decelerate0.05,0.7,0.1,1Entering. Things arrive gently.
--ease-accelerate0.3,0,0.8,0.15Exiting. Things leave briskly.
--ease-mechanical0.4,0,0.2,1Readouts, counters, segment switching.
--ease-detent0.34,1.28,0.64,1New. Sprung mechanisms only — encoder, toggle. Slight overshoot.
--ease-ballistic0.16,0.84,0.28,1New. Meters and needles. Damped, no overshoot.
--ease-liquidlinear(…)New. Surface tension — merge, level, elastic indicator, and every ambient loop in the system (see §02 E). Authored as linear(); a bezier can't carry the inflections.
Seven curves is the ceiling, and this is it. Past this a motion system stops being a system — nobody holds eight curves in their head and they start getting picked by feel. Liquid earned the seventh slot because linear() does something no bezier here can. An eighth means one of these is wrong.
