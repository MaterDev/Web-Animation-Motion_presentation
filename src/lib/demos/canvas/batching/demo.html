Three facts hold this picture together and all three are printable. Only three screens can sit at mutual 30° separation, so yellow takes the 15° collision — because it is the faintest of the four — and yellow runs at 108% of the others' frequency to suppress the residual beat. What comes out is a rosette: a controlled, least-objectionable moiré, which is what a press does on purpose where this deck's other sheets avoid moiré on purpose. The plates above are drifting, so you can watch the rosette break into a coarse beat as they leave their separation and re-form as they return: the angle is not a setting on a still picture, it is the variable, moving. Lock to standard angles stops it dead, which is why the standard set is standard. Dot gain is the other one: ink spreads into paper, so coverage is pushed by c + 0.12·sin(πc) — 12% at the midtone, nothing at either end. Turn it off and the whole page goes pale, which is what a plate looks like before the press has had it.
