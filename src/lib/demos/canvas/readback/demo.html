Drag on the image to set window and level — the two numbers every reading room argues about. They do not change the scan; they change which slice of its range the display spends its greys on, which is why the same pixels can show bone or soft tissue and never both at once. Left alone they sweep on their own, widening and sliding the way a pair of hands does while reading; the first drag takes them off it.

Nothing on the right is stored, and that is the point of the section. The histogram is counted out of the pixels every frame; the region statistics are computed from the pixels inside the box; the mark is assembled from the pixels above a threshold. Change the slice, the window, the level or the LUT and all of them follow, because none of them is a copy of the image — they are all questions asked of it. A retained-mode surface can tell you where its objects are and it cannot answer any of these, because the answer is not in the objects. It is in the pixels the objects turned into.
